GT2 Modern Physics

The port can drive the cars two ways. Classic is GT2's own physics, exactly: whole numbers (fixed point), 30 updates a second, every quirk kept. Modern is GT2's own car model rewritten in real-world units (meters, seconds, newtons) with decimal math, and run 120 times a second. Race rules, AI, HUD, sound and cameras are the same for both. This page shows where modern fits in each update, which GT2 habits it keeps and which it changes, and how it measures against classic.

Modern is the port's default. To play on GT2's own physics, set PSX_PHYSICS=classic before starting UGT2R (see the flags and settings guide). The numbers below are from the first version of modern physics; later changes to brakes, wheel mass and load transfer have their own pages.

In short. On the first version, 21 of 42 car checks landed inside their tolerances, plus the spread of AI finishing times; acceleration and braking came within about 2 to 5 % of GT2; AI laps ran 0 to 3 % faster. Modern was not tuned to match classic: it is GT2's own formulas, run more often and in real units.

Two strategies, one world

Classic

GT2's whole numbers: positions in 1/4096 m, angles in 4096ths of a turn, one update (a tick) every 2184/65536 s, about 1/30 s. GT2's order of steps, its timing and the order of its random draws are all kept. Classic is the yardstick: the comparisons below measure modern against it. The port's classic physics plays back recordings of real GT2 races identically.

Modern

Double-precision decimal numbers in meters, seconds, newtons and radians. The step rate can be set: 120, 150, 300 or 600 a second, any multiple of 30 that divides the race clock's 3000 units a second. The port uses 120.

Where modern sits in each update

The race itself still updates 30 times a second. Inside each update, the physics stages are handed to the modern model, which takes four small steps. Everything else runs the same code as classic. The boxes T1 to T16 are the stages of one update, in order.

What the modern step does

PartModern
HeaveThe body rides a spring-damper over the ground plane (2 Hz, damping 0.8), normal force clamped to 0–3 g; zero above ride height is airborne.
LoadsStatic per axle plus downforce, longitudinal and lateral transfer split by spring rates, lagged by 50 ms.
TiresGT2's own curves, GT2's slip ratio and low-speed fades; slip angle from atan(|v_lat| / |v_long|).
Brakes, assistsGT2's ABS, TCS and ASC laws, sampled at 30 Hz as in GT2 and held between samples.
Engine, drivetrainGT2's torque curve, friction, rev-limit fuel cut, turbo lag stages, clutch phases, gearbox rules and differentials, in SI units.
IntegrationEach wheel's spin is implicit in its own tire force; the body is semi-implicit Euler (velocity first, then position with the new velocity).
CollisionsCar pairs by separating axes, answered with an impulse (restitution 0.2, friction 0.3). Walls: each car's four corners swept from step start to end, so no tunneling at any speed (restitution 0.1, friction 0.35).
WearGT2's accumulator per second, on tarmac, outside the pit lane.

Every large difference, explained

  • Laps, 0 to 2.9 % faster. Smoother loads from the S1 suspension give more mean grip; the classic car also hit walls more (the TVR at Grindelwald: 4 hits against 0).
  • Braking, −5 to +2 %. With M1 and M3 kept the stop is GT2's within 5 %; the rest is the 120 Hz wheel and ABS loop settling nearer the brake peak.
  • 4WD launch, +5.9 % on 0–100. The Lancer's center coupling is modeled as a split plus a capped coupling; GT2 uses a locked shaft that slips at 784 N·m. A later version could model the locked mode exactly.
  • A 6-car Red Rock Valley race is 8 % shorter on modern (192.6 s against 210.4 s for 2 laps): Red Rock Valley is fast and banked, and a pack in the slipstream adds to the oval effect.

The kei car in the Machine Test

A test run saw the Mira TR-XX take 2:15 for 400 m in the Machine Test with the AI driving. Classic does exactly that: 135.2 s for 400 m, 31.6 km/h top speed. Modern does nearly the same (84.9 s, 37.8 km/h). It is GT2's behavior, not a physics bug.

Machine Test events cap the AI at 75 % throttle (the AI throttle cap is 3072 out of 4096 for every class). The Mira is turbocharged (0.2 bar at 7,500 rpm). At three-quarter throttle its turbo lag state settles below the engine speed, so the delivered throttle is about 0.6, and at the launch revs (about 6,500 rpm) the engine's torque barely exceeds its friction. The launch clutch passes 1.125 × almost nothing, and the car creeps until the clutch engages. Scripted at full throttle it runs 0–400 m in 22.3 s on both strategies.

So the cause is the Machine Test rules and the AI, not either physics.

Determinism

  • The step uses only +, −, ×, ÷ and sqrt, which give the same result on every computer, and its own sin, cos and atan2 instead of the system's: the same numbers on Windows, Linux and in the browser.
  • No threads, no wall clock, no hash-ordered iteration, no random draws in the physics.
  • A test runs one 6-car race with every car AI-driven, 2,400 updates, twice: every value of the whole race is exactly equal. Two different builds of the port also produce exactly the same states.
  • This is what lets replays and ghosts store only a seed and inputs (see the replays page).
Percentages are modern minus classic, divided by classic. Laps and Machine Tests are driven by GT2's AI; straight-line runs are scripted.
