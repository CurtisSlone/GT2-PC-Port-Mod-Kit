GT2 Simulation · how the physics works

Inside GT2's Physics

What the game actually computes while you race, checked step by step against runs of the real game. GT2 updates its physics 30 times a second; each update is called a tick. Charts come from scripted test runs on stock cars. Each finding says whether it was measured in the game or only read from the game's code.

Some findings name a memory address (like 0x80046EA4, a location in the PlayStation's RAM) or an offset (like car+0x792, a byte position inside the car's record in memory). They are there for modders; you can skip them.

Braking

Full brake at the end of each car's straight, so start speeds differ. The table gives the distance from 100 km/h to a stop, read from the same runs.

The rubber band

AI cars don't get extra grip or power. Their physics clock runs faster or slower: each tick they live more or less time. Fitted over every tick of three 6-car races; each event sets its own ramp. The rubber band has its own page.

How we know it is time: for every AI tick, the distance moved divided by (the car's own speed × 1/30 s) equals its time scale within 0.001. Its grip values never change. Holding one AI car at 1.5× took it from 5th to 1st in 20 s; holding it at −1 (0xFFFF) leaves it parked on the grid. The slow side is much stronger than the speed-up, which is why the field stays packed.

What tune-ups do

200 tune rows (208 runs: each change plus 8 stock bases) on copies of a save card, one part or setting changed at a time, same scripted inputs. A few highlights.

The findings

Measured = checked tick by tick against runs of the real game, with zero differences. Code read = read from the game's code, not yet seen in a test run.
