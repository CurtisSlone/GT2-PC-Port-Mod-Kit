The engine note: recordings, resampled

A player car plays two layers: an intake/engine layer and an exhaust layer. Each is a sound file holding 5 to 8 short loops recorded at points along the rev range. Every opponent plays one generic file instead (a non-turbo or a turbo one), whatever its car.

recording Arecording B

Schematic, not a real file's numbers. Between two recordings both play, with an equal-power (sine) crossfade, each at its own level.

pitch = recorded pitch x rpm / recorded rpm x Doppler
  • Nothing else moves the pitch. No throttle term, no table. The rev limiter, gear changes and the launch are heard only through the rpm the physics computes (the limiter as the rpm bouncing 500 below the limit; an upshift as a drop of about 1,000 to 1,300 rpm over four ticks).
  • Mufflers and turbo kits swap recordings. The exhaust layer comes from one of four muffler-stage files, or four turbo files. Nothing is filtered or pitched.
  • Levels come from two bytes the physics writes each tick (a tick is one physics step, 1/30 s): a load value (throttle distance from 3/8, louder in low gears) for the engine layer and the throttle for the exhaust layer.
  • Change limits: pitch can change at most an octave per frame, volume at most a quarter of full scale.

On the disc: 305 sets of 9 files, plus the two generic opponent files. 1,026 of the 2,745 set files (in 214 sets) are a placeholder, a 2-sample dummy, because each set only records the variants its cars can actually fit. The race cars' sets have only one turbo file, since no parts can be bought for them. Of all 618 cars, exactly one can reach a placeholder: see the Demio bug below.

Distance, pan and Doppler

player-scale carsopponents

Volume = 0x80000 / (meters + 1), capped at full; opponents 0x18000 / (meters + 1). Drawn from those formulas.

  • Distance is measured from the camera, not from your car.
  • Pan comes from the camera's right axis through an equal-power table.
  • Doppler uses a speed of sound of 5.67 m a frame, which is 170 m/s at 30 frames a second: about half the real value, so the effect is doubled. It switches off while the camera moves faster than half that speed.
  • Riding view: when the camera rides a car, that car is at full volume, centered, with no Doppler.
  • Opponents also play their engine layer at 0.8.

Tires, turbo, impacts

Besides the engine, each car has four loops and two one-shots. Each loop is the sum of that car's wheels on one surface.

No sound exists for grass, sand or gravel (surfaces 2 to 4), for bottoming out, for wall scrapes, or for contacts between other cars. There is no announcer on this disc. In replays: no crash sounds, squeal x 3 for every car, opponents at the player's distance scale, no race music.

24 voices, shared by priority

The PlayStation can play 24 sounds (voices) at once. Each car's sounds get priorities 6 x rank + 0 to 4, where rank 1 is the camera's car and the rest follow in order of distance from the camera. A lower number is more important. A new sound takes a free voice, or steals one playing at its own or a less important priority.

Quirk: between candidates of different priorities, the last one checked wins, not the least important one. Harmless while voices are plentiful.

Music and volume

  • Race music is CD audio (XA): a random one of six race tunes from the countdown's "go", with jingles before some starts and at the finish.
  • Menu music is set per GT Mode page by a byte in the page and changes only when the track differs.
  • Two options scale everything, both 0 to 255: sound effects (loops and one-shots are scaled twice, so they fall off as v² / 255²) and music.

Lap timing finer than a tick

The race clock moves in 1/30 s ticks, but lap times are not rounded to a tick. When a car crosses the line, the game works out how far into the tick the crossing happened from the distances before and after.

  • Lap time = this crossing minus the previous one. Both are truncated, so the error is under 1 ms. Sector lines and the Machine Test's 400 m and 1,000 m marks use the same rule.
  • The first crossing from the grid only starts lap 1. A backward crossing counts nothing until the car crosses forward again.
  • The banner, lap list and results show the saved values. The running clocks add 0 to 16 ms of dither so the milliseconds keep moving between 33 ms ticks. That display can read up to 16 ms ahead and is never saved.
  • A 99-lap race of the long kinds also ends at 2 hours on the clock.

Bugs in the original game

Demio turbo plays a dummy

The Mazda Demio A-spec '97 with a turbo kit has no turbo recordings: its exhaust layer plays the 2-sample placeholder, 1.65x its real pitch at 3,000 rpm and 2.9x (1.5 octaves) at 7,000. The only one of 618 cars that can reach a placeholder. A data slip, not a logic one.

engine/00010_t0..t3.es

"HP" is really PS

Peak power is computed with the 716.2 constant, which gives metric PS, and printed next to an "HP" that is part of the page picture. 1 PS = 0.9863 hp.

car spec pages

"in." is really mm

Ride height values are the set-up's millimeter bytes, printed next to an "in." in the page picture. The values are right; the label is wrong.

set-up +0x5C / +0x5D

Speed records print 123.5 for 123.05

Hundredths are formatted with "%d.%d" instead of "%d.%02d", so a leading zero is lost. Affects the record panel, the Max Speed test and the results.

speed record text

Locked wheels squeal like spinning ones

MEASURED

A dropped minus sign keeps the slip ratio positive, so the lock factor never applies and braking lock-ups use the wheelspin factor. This reading matches all 93,576 wheel updates measured in a real race.

Silent crashes and off-road

The crash sound plays only for the camera's car; hits between other cars are silent. Grass, sand and gravel have squeal values but no loop plays them.

crash and surface sounds

Not yet confirmed: one boost gauge face serves both needle scales, so a car with more than 3.0 bar may read half on the face.
