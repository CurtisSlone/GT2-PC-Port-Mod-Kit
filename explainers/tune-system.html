01

What a tune is

In GT mode every car is one record in the car database that points at 27 part records: brakes, brake controller, steering, chassis, weight reduction, racing modify, engine, port polish, engine balance, displacement, computer, NA tune, turbo, drivetrain, flywheel, clutch, propeller shaft, LSD, gearbox, suspension, intercooler, muffler, front tires, rear tires, ASC, TCS and wheels. A stock part has price 0.

Your tune is a 0x84-byte (132-byte) set-up record stored with the garage car (and copied into the race entry). It holds those part indices plus every setting you can adjust: gear ratios, final drive, auto level, downforce, camber, ride height, toe, springs, damper steps, stabilizer steps, LSD values, brake controller steps, ASC and TCS steps. Fitting a part just replaces its index in the set-up with the index of that part's record for your car (or the generic car 00000 record if your car has none), then loads that part's default settings.

At race start the game turns the set-up into a 0x1C0-byte (448-byte) race spec, and the race code turns the spec into the car's physics record. The physics never looks at the parts again. Offsets like spec +0x1B0 below are byte positions inside these records.

The highlighted boxes are the two records worth knowing: the set-up is what you change in the shop and what the memory card stores; the race spec is what the physics is built from.

02

Copy, scale, add: how a part changes a number

The race spec is built by small lists of instructions stored in the game's program, one list per part kind. Each instruction names a source byte in the part record, a destination in the spec and one of five operations. Nothing about a part's effect is hard-coded beyond these lists, the power product and a few lines for weight.

OpWhat it doesUsed by, for example
copyreplaces the spec value with the part's valueengine curve, gear ratios, brake torque, tire compound, suspension defaults, LSD kind
x %/100spec = spec x part / 100 (signed bytes, truncating)flywheel, clutch, propeller shaft on inertias; racing modify on weight, yaw and roll
x ‰/1000spec = spec x part / 1000weight reduction on curb weight
add eachadds one part value to every element of an arrayengine balance and NA tune shift every rpm point of the engine curve
addadds one value to one fieldengine balance, NA tune and turbo raise the rev limit (NA and turbo the redline too)

Settings with steps (dampers, stabilizers, brake controller, ASC, TCS) go through one interpolation: value = lo + (hi - lo) x (step - 1) / (steps - 1), rounded toward lo.

03

Engine and power parts

Eight parts carry a power percentage. The game multiplies them all together into one number, the upgrade power in permille (thousandths; spec +0x1B0). A few also reshape the rev range. Nothing else about the engine curve's shape changes when you buy them.

The power formula

upgrade power (permille) = 1000 x (1 + p1/100) x (1 + p2/100) x ... x (1 + p8/100)

game's exact form:  mult = 1000
                    for each of the 8 power kinds, in table order:
                        pm   = (byte == 255) ? 519 : byte + 100
                        mult = mult * pm / 100        (integer, truncates each step)

Table order is port polish, engine balance, displacement, computer, NA tune, turbo, muffler, intercooler. A stock part has 0 % (factor 1). The byte 255 is a special case that means x5.19.

The product is not applied flat. The engine curve is scaled by a ramp: at the first rpm point the factor is the turbo's low-rpm power % (100 % if there is none), and it rises in a straight line, by rpm, to the full product at the last rpm point. On top of that every point gets the engine's own hidden multiplier (spec +0x1AE; see below). Then the turbo boost shape is applied.

Worked example (R34 Skyline GT-R). The garage shows 280 ps. The engine record's hidden multiplier is 116 %, and the torque curve times 1.16 makes about 330 ps (280 x 1.16, plus rounding). With every upgrade fitted the product is x2.572, and the curve reaches 785 ps at 7600 rpm, before the turbo shaping. That is less than 330 x 2.572 because of the ramp: only the last rpm point gets the whole product.

The units check out: the curve's peak (torque x 9.8 x rpm) equals the shown power on non-turbo cars (Mustang SVT Cobra '99: 324.0 ps computed, 324 shown).

04

Power multiplier calculator

Type the power percentage byte of each fitted part (0 = not fitted). This computes the product exactly as the game does, then the ramp the engine curve is scaled by. It is the multiplier before turbo shaping, not a power figure. The part percentages themselves live in each car's part records on your disc; none are included here.

1000spec +0x1B0, permille (exact integer)
x1.000upgrade product
x1.000curve factor at the first rpm point
x1.000curve factor at the last rpm point

Factor at each rpm point = engine multiplier x (low-rpm % + (product - low-rpm %) x (rpm - first rpm) / (last rpm - first rpm)). Per-part truncation follows the table order above. The real torque curve then also passes through the turbo boost shape, which is not part of this calculator.

05

Weight reduction and the mass floor

Weight reduction 1-3 (#18-20) scales the curb weight and the yaw inertia by a permille byte, and the roll inertia by a percent byte. Racing modify (#34) multiplies weight, yaw and roll again by its own percent bytes. So weight = chassis kg x ‰/1000 x %/100.

The physics then applies a mass floor: 900 kg and up is kept as is; 750 to 900 kg is squeezed into 810 to 900 kg; anything under 750 kg counts as 810 kg. A very light car gains less from losing weight than the menu suggests. Yaw and roll inertia also have a floor (inertia byte 30, i.e. 3000 kg·m² of yaw).

Measured on the RX-7 GT-C, 0-100 km/h: 6.66 s stock; weight reduction 1 / 2 / 3: 6.59 / 6.52 / 6.57 s, top speed 242 / 243 / 245 km/h. Lancer Evo VI: 5.66 / 5.58 / 5.50 s. Stage 3 on the RX-7 is slower off the line than stage 2 in that one scripted run; the test does not say why.

mass the physics usescatalog weight (no floor)

Flywheel, clutch and propeller shaft

These three parts change no power at all. They multiply rotating inertias (and the engine drag term) by percent bytes. A lighter engine spins up faster between shifts, which is where most of their gain comes from.

Clutch 1-3#3-5engine inertia, engine drag, front and rear wheel inertia; also copies one more byte (spec +0x33, meaning unknown)RX-7 6.66 → 6.60 / 6.45 / 6.19 s; Lancer 5.80 → 5.71 / 5.54 / 5.31 s

Engine inertia is 0.30 kg·m² on road cars and 0.17-0.19 on race cars; flywheel 3 took one test car from 0.29 to 0.20. The flywheel rows gain more than the inertia alone predicts (flywheel 3: -0.50 s measured vs -0.19 s from mass alone) because the lighter engine also shortens every shift.

07

Gearbox, final drive and auto level

The gearbox part (#13-15) copies the gear count, the ratios (x1000, reverse and 1st to 7th), the final drive and its range, an "adjustable" flag and the auto level. The set-up holds your eight ratios, the final drive and the auto level. In the physics each overall ratio is gear x final.

Stock example (R34): 3.827 / 2.360 / 1.685 / 1.312 / 1.000 / 0.793, final 3.545, reverse 3.280, matching the real car.

The sports gearboxes store no ratios

All three gearbox stages store 0xFFFF in place of ratios (stages 1 and 2 have a fixed final drive and auto level, stage 3 has ranges). The game computes them: the top gear reaches the rev limit at the auto level x 10 km/h on the driven wheel, each lower gear is one step shorter, the steps growing down the box (a table per gear count), and reverse is 3/4 first + 1/4 second. This happens when the box is fitted, when the car enters the garage (which also sets set-up flag 0x80: "ratios held in the set-up"), and at race start for set-ups without that flag (opponents, license and arcade cars).

A limit of the test runs below: the gearbox rows ran with the stock box's ratios, so gearbox 1-3 and both auto-level rows show no change; only the final-drive rows are real. The game's own fit gives, for example, the RX-7 stage-1 box 2.909 / 1.954 / 1.412 / 1.078 / 0.869 (stock 3.483 / 2.015 / 1.391 / 1.000 / 0.762).

Final drive, measured. At the top of its range: RX-7 top speed 240 → 212 km/h, Lancer 233 → 199 km/h. At the bottom: Lancer 0-100 km/h 5.80 → 7.36 s, while the RX-7 gets faster (6.66 → 6.01 s): with the stock ratios and a 2.5 final its new 1st is about its old 2nd, so it reaches 100 km/h with one shift instead of two; the Lancer's new 1st is taller than its old 3rd. Top speed itself (car +0x370) is the flat-road speed where drag and rolling resistance meet the power, not the rev-limit speed.

08

Limited-slip differentials

The LSD part (#21-25) copies a kind for the front and the rear axle, then each axle's initial, acceleration and deceleration values (default, min, max). Your settings in the set-up are the six values (initial F/R, accel F/R, decel F/R).

The kind bytes are letters. 102, 104, 109, 110 and 118 are the ASCII codes for f h m n v, and the race code looks each one up in the string f r n m h v y t (stored at 0x80046CEC while racing) to get a differential type 0-7.

6yopen + active yaw bias (the Lancer's rear)LSD values stored, bias gain from the assist logic

clutch torque (types 3, 4) = max(initial, accel x drive torque)     on power
                           = max(initial, decel x |drive torque|)   off power
initial: kgf·m x 9.8 x 4 (the physics' 1/4 N·m unit);  accel, decel: value / 100

The layout also fixes up the types: on a rear-drive car the front becomes 0 and an unset rear becomes open; on FF the reverse; on 4WD both unset axles become open. Types 3 and 4 (m and h) behave the same in the differential; what the two letters were meant to tell apart is a guess.

Measured, World Test Lot full-lock corner, mean lateral g: RX-7 LSD 1-4 0.942 → 0.944 / 0.933 / 0.953 / 0.953. Lancer Evo VI LSD 4: 0.664 → 1.01 (it replaces the stock active rear). Lancer LSD 5: no change.

10

Brakes and the brake controller

Brakes (#1) copy three bytes: front torque, rear torque and the handbrake torque (rear wheels). Each step is 98 N·m (10 kgf·m). The front/rear split of braking comes only from those two torques.

Brake controller (#2) is not a balance in the physics. Its front and rear steps become the per-axle ABS slip gain. ABS runs on every car, all the time; the brake demand never asks for more torque than the tire can pass, and is released in proportion to how far the wheel slips past the tire's peak.

demand = brake pedal x clamp( (grip x radius / brake torque)
                              x (1 - gain x (slip - peak slip)), 0, 1 )

Measured, 100-0 km/h: brakes on the RX-7 30.7 → 30.7 m and on the Lancer 29.6 → 29.6 m: more torque changes nothing when the tire is the limit. Brake controller at its ends: RX-7 30.7 → 31.7 / 31.3 / 31.2 / 30.6 m. Grip parts together (the "grip" full tune): RX-7 30.7 → 25.1 m, Lancer 29.6 → 24.6 m. The ABS formula matched the game on 47,132 measured wheel steps.

Tires: compounds, grip bytes and the dirt tire

Tires 1-7 (#39-45) pick a front and a rear tire record. Each points at a size and a compound. The compound holds one grip byte and the tire's curves: an 8-point slip-angle curve (cornering), two 6+6-point slip-ratio curves (drive and braking, with a cap on cornering grip), a load curve and a camber curve.

For the tire curves the axle grip is grip byte / 100 x the chassis grip % (AI cars also get the event's AI grip %). The same byte also scales tire wear through a second formula centered on 175. The real dirt tire is stage 7 (grip byte 200), the one the rally events fit.

12

Racing modify and downforce

Racing modify (#34) swaps the body (a race-car look by car ID), scales weight, yaw and roll inertia, copies the drag coefficient (Cd x100; R34 0.35), the front and rear downforce range (Cl x100), the front and rear track and the width (frontal area). The downforce settings in the set-up are the two Cl values.

frontal area A   = 0.95 x width x height
drag coefficient = 0.5 x 0.8 x A x Cd   +  10 % of (front + rear downforce coefficients)
downforce/axle   = 0.5 x 0.8 x A x Cl x speed²   (cut by slipstream, down to 25 % in a full draft)

The air density in effect is 0.8 kg/m³, not the real 1.2. Downforce adds drag: 10 % of its sum is added to the drag term.

Racing modify's track widths never reach the physics. At race start the game overwrites the track and the collision box with values measured from the car's 3D model (R34: 1480/1490 mm in the database, 1424/1498 mm in the race).

Measured on the Lancer Evo VI (racing modify fitted): racing modify itself 0-100 km/h 5.80 → 5.70 s, top speed 233 → 231 km/h. Rear downforce at minimum / maximum: top speed 231 → 236 / 226 km/h; front downforce barely moved it.

13

ASC and TCS

Both kits are generic parts (car 00000), so any car can fit them. Their steps are interpolated into the spec and from there into four assist values.

TCS (#38)

Cuts the throttle when the worst driven wheel spins past the tire's drive-side peak slip, scaled down as lateral g rises (less wheelspin allowed in a corner) and relieved by steering. The kit's default step is level 0, which is off.

throttle x= clamp(1 + K x (worst slip - r x peak slip), 0, 1)

ASC (#0)

Adds brake to the outer front wheel when an oversteer measure (body slip angle x speed) passes a threshold. A higher level byte means a later intervention. The kit's default is its top step, where the threshold sits at the measure's ceiling (4096), so in every test run it never acted.

outer front brake += 8 x gain x (oversteer - threshold)

Some race cars carry an ASC level stock.

How the drivetrain settings reach the physics

At race start the drive layout and the AWD mode from the drivetrain record become one drive code. On a dirt course (course flag 4) every 4WD car is switched to code 6.

CodeLayout / AWD modeHow torque is split
0FR, MR, RRrear axle only
1FFfront axle only
24WD, mode 0front-driven; a viscous coupling sends k x (front - rear speed) to the rear
34WD, mode 1stiff center: shared mean speed, the front-rear difference relaxes
44WD, mode 2fixed front share; slips at 784 N·m (80 kgf·m), locks again when speeds meet
54WD, mode 3 (R34)front share grows with rear slip and falls with yaw (1 to 0.25); locked on launch, hard braking or overrun; limit 1470 N·m
64WD, mode 4 / any 4WD on dirtfixed open front share (planetary style)

Each axle then gets its differential type from the LSD part (section 08), and the transmission choice (automatic or manual) comes from the race entry; AI cars are always automatic. The assists read the spec's ASC and TCS values.

The port's own drivetrain code (clutch, gearbox, center coupling, differentials, the 1- and 2-speed boxes, engine output and rev limiter) gives the same results as the original game, compared over more than six million physics steps.

The test runs

Each part and setting was changed one at a time on a copy of a save, raced with the same scripted inputs as the stock car, and followed from the set-up to the race spec to the car's first different physics step.

  • 200 tests: 94 on the Mazda RX-7 GT-C '99 (turbo FR), 103 on the Lancer Evolution VI GSR '99 (4WD), 3 on the MR2 G-Limited '98 (NA tune).
  • Four scripted tests: launch on a straight, braking from speed, a full-lock corner and a dirt run (75 launch, 16 braking, 108 corner, 1 dirt).
  • 178 tests change the car's behavior; 22 are identical, each explained: ASC and TCS kits at their defaults, brake controller at its default, gearbox 1-3 and auto level (stock ratios, see 07), Lancer LSD 5, and the Lancer dirt test (the event swapped back to stock tires).

One part at a time: 0-100 km/h, RX-7 GT-C

seconds gained against stock 6.66 s (left of zero = slower)
