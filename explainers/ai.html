Inside GT2's AI

How the opponents in GT Mode drive, measured on real races. The short version: each AI car follows a racing line drawn into the course file, aims at a point ahead of it, and works its own pedals and steering the way a player's pad would. The physics after that is the same physics the player's car runs.

"car+0x5C" style labels below are byte offsets inside a car's record in memory (2,880 bytes per car). "AI table +0x02" is an offset inside the small AI table built at race start.

One driver, the same inputs as a player

GT2's race code steps every car 30 times a second (one "tick") through a fixed list of stages. The AI lives in one of them. At stage A6 (inputs), a player's car reads the pad; an AI car runs the AI driver instead. The driver writes the same values the pad would: steering, throttle, brake, clutch and a reverse request. Everything after A6 (traction control, ABS, the drivetrain, moving the car) is the same as for the player, unchanged.

The AI reads this tick's time step and front slip angles, and last tick's everything else: position, speed, the proximity zones and closing alert from the collision stage (B4), and its race state from stage G. The rubber band (K) sets the AI car's time scale for the next tick, so a car held back or pushed forward lives more or less time per tick, steering included.

A line follower, not a planner

Each course file carries up to seven precomputed lines made of straights and constant-radius arcs: a main racing line, two passing lines, a side selector, a pit-entry line and the pit lane with the boxes. No speeds, widths or brake markers are stored. Every target speed is worked out from the arc's radius, the bank, the surface and the car.

Steering: aim at a point ahead

This method is called "pure pursuit". The wanted yaw rate (how fast the car should turn) goes through a one-sided controller: the AI steers K × (wanted − actual yaw rate) only while the car turns less than wanted. An over-rotating car gets no counter-steer from it. The angle is clamped at the tire's best slip angle (not on dirt), the mean front slip angle is added, and the wheel then moves toward that target at the car's own steering rate.

How far ahead it looks

The lookahead grows with speed and tops out at 90 km/h in every row: 10 to 48 m for rear-drive cars, 17 to 45 m for front-drive and AWD, 20 to 40 m on dirt. The game overrides it in special cases: 5 m in the pit lane, 20 m entering the pit, 10 m while reversing, 40 m while recovering from a spin.

Corner speeds and braking points

Corner speed from the arc and its own grip

At each node the AI looks up the next segment. A straight's target is the car's top-speed estimate. An arc's target comes from its radius and the car's own sideways grip on that surface, raised when the road is banked into the turn, plus an offset, kept between 20 km/h and top speed. On tarmac k = 1.05: the AI aims 5 % above the steady cornering speed of its own grip.

v = k · √( R · a_lat · surface grip · wear / (1 − sin|bank|) ) + offset
k = 1.05 tarmac, 1.00 dirt (+5 km/h offset)
car+0x5C, car+0x370 · AI table +0x02, +0x04

Brake when the stopping distance runs out

The AI compares the distance it needs to slow to the next arc's speed with the straight-line distance to the next node. The deceleration is the smaller of what the brakes and what the grip on that surface allow, adjusted for slope. Each unit of closing alert (a car close ahead) adds 20 m. When the need reaches the distance, it brakes.

need = (v² − v_next²) / (2 · a_brake) · (1 − sin pitch) + 20 m × alert
car+0x62, car+0x44C · AI table +0x00, +0x26

Pedals: full, proportional, or all-or-nothing

On a straight the throttle is full. In a corner a proportional controller holds the segment's speed: 0.75 throttle at 2 km/h under the target, 0.5 less per m/s faster. Braking for the next corner uses the same controller, stiffer. The brake is either off or fully on; ABS, which every car runs, shapes it. The AI never feathers the brake pedal.

corner:  throttle = 0.75 − 0.5 per m/s over (target − 2 km/h)
braking: −2 per m/s (from a straight), −1 (from a corner)
brake:   0 or 100 %, only once throttle hits 0 above target

It holds the gearbox while it feathers

Each time the speed controller gives a partial throttle it bumps the car's shift timer by one. The automatic gearbox does not decide a shift while that timer is above zero, so an AI car that is feathering the throttle never changes gear.

partial throttle → shift timer + 1   (car+0x648)

Racecraft is three rules

  1. Closing on a car ahead (the alert is on): take a passing line on the free side, preferring the one the coming arc favors. If neither side is free, lift by the alert: half throttle at half alert, none at full.
  2. A car alongside on one side: move to the line away from it.
  3. Nobody in the five forward and side zones: go back to the main racing line.

That is all. The AI does not look at other cars' lines and plans nothing beyond the next node. Line changes happen at once: the target point jumps to the new line and the steering ramp smooths the move. The two passing lines are drawn beside the main line, and the selector line marks the stretches where they change sides. In one two-lap race on Grindelwald with five AI cars there were 53 line changes.

A car on the pit-lane line is invisible to the collision check: no zones, no draft, no contact.

Spins, starts, pit stops and the finish

States 2 → 3 → 4 → 0 are the pit stop: in an endurance race the car moves over lines 6 → 3 → 4 → 3 → 6. State 6 (a stop at the end of a license test) also leads to parked.

Recovery

Decided from the heading error and the wall contacts alone. More than 80° off the line direction: recover, with a long 40 m lookahead. Both front corners against a wall: reverse. Reversing ends once the car points within 10° of the line; recovering ends within 40° above 30 km/h.

The start

On the grid the AI blips the throttle, holds the revs near the redline for the last 0.7 s, and lets the clutch in once the revs pass ¾ of the redline. For the first 3 s it does not steer while it is on a straight segment.

Pit stops only for tires

An AI car pits only when tire wear is on and one tire falls under the low-grip threshold, and never on its last lap. It follows dedicated pit lines at fixed speeds (102, 79 and 20 km/h), enters the lane 25 m before its box if it is under 100 km/h, stops for 5 s, and rejoins at the lane's end. Car i's box is pit-lane node 8 − i.

The player's car uses it too

Whenever the player's race state is not "racing" (the cool-down lap after the finish, the pit lane) the same driver takes over the player's car. That is why your car drives itself through the pits in an endurance race.

Difficulty is data

There is no global difficulty switch in the driver. At race start the game builds a small AI table from five built-in 16-byte rows in the race code, one per drive type plus one used by every car on dirt. Each event then scales it. A few of the row values:

Two surprises

The rally "AI" is a ghost

In the GT Mode rally events the opponent is not the AI driver. It is a recorded run, stored with the event, played back as pad input through the same steps as the player's controls, with no contacts. The physics simulates it again every time. The AI takes over only after the ghost's finish, to brake it to a stop: in one rally it took over at tick 2,636 and parked the car 129 ticks later. (This is also why the Tahiti Maze opponent can crash at the start; see bugs, B6.)

No shortcuts for distant cars

There is no simplified mode for cars far from the camera. With the player's car parked on the grid while the five AI cars raced up to 7.7 km of course away (two laps of Grindelwald), every AI car still got its full driver step on every one of 7,304 ticks.
