How GT2 Draws a Picture

What Gran Turismo 2 does between a car's position in the world and the pixels on the screen. Every number here comes from the game's code or from the game's own values in a recorded race. It explains the wobble, the shimmer and the pop-in PlayStation players know.

Thirty times a second, in five steps

The PlayStation has no depth buffer and no floating point. GT2 sorts every polygon into one of 4,096 depth bins and sends the whole frame to the GPU as one linked list, drawn from the farthest bin to the nearest. The CPU builds frame N while the GPU paints frame N−1.

1 · FRAMEFlip and sendLast frame's list goes to the GPU by DMA. The ordering table and the primitive pool switch halves; the new half is cleared by the DMA engine.
2 · VIEWCamera to GTE blockThe camera becomes 104 bytes of fixed-point constants: a rotation scaled by the window, a translation, the screen center, the focal length and four frustum planes.
3 · RENDERProject, cull, binHand-written machine code pushes every corner point (vertex) through the GTE, the PlayStation's geometry coprocessor that does the 3D math, culls faces against the planes, clips the ones that cross them, and links each polygon into a depth bin.
4 · 2DHUD and textSprites and flat polygons at fixed bins: the tachometer, the lap panel, every glyph of text (two sprites each), the pause panel, the fades.
5 · SYNCWait for the pictureDrawSync polls the GPU, VSync waits for the next field. These waits are where the game's clock ticks for the drawing code.

How big is the drawing code?

227,888 bytes: 28.9% of the game's 770 KB of code. A third of it is hand-written machine code, all of it in the mesh renderers (the parts that draw 3D models).

Not counted: the camera logic, physics code that happens to use the GTE, menu logic behind the page engine, and the unused Arcade-menu code (25 KB, never run on this disc).

The renderers are hand-written

Seven functions carry the 3D: two course section renderers (23 KB), two trackside-object renderers (23 KB), two car body renderers (22 KB) and the ground-plane shadow renderer. They were written by hand in machine code, not compiled. Together with the two polygon clippers they are 76 KB of the 228.

From a point in the world to a pixel

The view set-up turns the camera into a block of integers. The GTE multiplies, divides once, and truncates. No sub-pixel survives: that truncation is the wobble players know.

What the block holds

+0x00
rotation: window scale S times the transposed camera matrix
+0x14
translation: that rotation applied to minus the camera position
+0x2C, +0x40
four frustum planes, stored where the GTE's light and color matrices go (GT2 never lights anything, so it borrows them)
+0x54, +0x58
OFX = W/2, OFY = h/2
+0x5C
H; +0x62 the near plane = H/2
+0x78 (view)
lod_zoom = 160 x 4096 / H: how large things look, used by every distance rule

The planes carry a 16-pixel guard band, so objects just off screen are still drawn: 7% of the polygons GT2 submits lie wholly outside the picture. The race view's picture is 1.1 times wider per metre than it is tall, because 320 x 240 pixels show a 320 x 264 window.

Sixteen bits are not enough, so GT2 shrinks the world

The GTE multiplies 16-bit vertices. A car's model counts in 1/2048 m, so anything farther than 8 m would overflow. Before each object the game measures how many bits its distance plus its size needs and drops the excess: one bit of vertex precision, and one bit of the rotation matrix, per doubling of distance.

e = max(bits(|t| + extent) - scale - 2, 0), sh = scale - 12 + e; the translation is shifted by sh, the 4.12 rotation by e. The shimmer of distant cars is this: their matrix has 7 fractional bits left at 256 m.

Depth bins

Every polygon gets one depth (its farthest or nearest corner) and goes into bin (SZ << sh) >> 13. Because the shift undoes the shrink, a bin is always 0.125 m of camera depth. 4,096 bins cover 512 m; everything farther lands in the last bin, drawn first and unsorted.

Two polygons in the same bin keep their insertion order: that is the flicker where a car's shadow, drawn by its own bin and not against the road, darkens a neighboring car.

What gets drawn at all

Nothing is drawn by distance alone. The course has hand-made visibility lists; cars and trackside objects have level tables compared against an apparent-size measure. The thresholds scale with the camera's zoom: a telephoto replay camera keeps detail far longer than the chase view.

Rule: LOD i while d < maxdist_i x H / 160, with maxdist 5 / 15 / 300 m in every car file. Fitted from the game's own choices over 7,822 frames of a race. Wheels: full under 8.1 m, 8-gon under 16 m, hexagon under 32 m, none beyond (H 216). The player's own car is always LOD 0.

Course: lists, not distance

  • Each track section carries a hand-made list of the sections to draw from it (a median of 17; 256 at most). Nothing off the list is ever drawn: the list is the draw distance.
  • Listed sections are box-tested against the frustum, sorted by an octagonal distance (max + mid/2 + min/4) and drawn; level-3 entries draw only their lamp glows.
  • Trackside objects use the same apparent-size measure against a per-model level table; past the last level the object vanishes.
  • The mirror draws the low-detail mesh, cars within 100 m, no objects and no sky mesh.

No light, no fog

The GTE's lighting commands are never used; shade is painted into vertex colors and the cars' palettes are dimmed per course. The fog path exists in all four model renderers but is switched off: they set the depth-cue factor to zero before using it. The one moving highlight is the car's reflection, a 128 x 128 sphere map looked up by the camera-space normal: u = 64 + 64 n.x, v = 64 + 64 n.y, and it fades not with distance but with the dirt a car has picked up.

Six cars, nailed down in video memory

A car's texture lives in a 64 x 224 slot and its four palette variants (open, shaded, dark, black) in four rows under it. Slot k = 26 − i: car i sits at (640 − 64 i, 256). A seventh car would land at x 256, inside the frame buffer.

Texture rect x = (k & 15) << 6, y = (k << 4) & 0x100, 64 x 224; palette rows at y + 0xE0 + 4 j. Models go to six 20 KB slots in RAM, and the car draw list's head word sits right after the sixth car record. Course pages are 4-bit only; GT2 never reads VRAM back and never uses 15-bit textures.

Text: two sprites per glyph, with kerning

Every letter on the HUD, the card screens and the boot "Loading Save Data..." screen comes from one routine: a sprite from the glyph's main image, an optional second sprite for an accent, both linked at the font's ordering-table entry. The advance to the next glyph is kerned from a bit-packed pair table.

Three fonts

  • This sprite font, used in races, the card screens and the boot screen.
  • A fixed-pitch text stream, with an unused kanji (Japanese character) drawer.
  • The GT Mode menu font: 12-byte image records, no kerning.

In the port

The classic look shows GT2's own picture, made the way this page describes. The remaster look draws the course and cars with a new renderer from the game's own scene data, at a higher resolution, so the whole-pixel wobble, the 12.5 cm depth bins and the warped road textures do not apply there. The game still decides what is in the scene: the same cars, the same detail levels and visibility lists.
