GT2 port · modern physics · brake feel

Why the brakes keep modern straight

The same 300ZX-GTS GT, driven through the same corner on classic and on modern physics. Classic rotates under trail braking (braking while turning in) and then fishtails on the throttle. Modern first stayed straight. The cause is how each physics splits the brake force between the four wheels, not the tires or the springs. The numbers below come from rollouts: copies of the classic run, switched to modern at one moment and left to drive on with the same recorded inputs. The last sections show the change that came out of it: brakes at real size.

The same law, two outcomes

Both physics use GT2's brake law: each wheel's torque is the pedal times the axle's torque, cut by the ABS so that it never asks more than the tire's grip times its radius. GT2's torques are large (10.4 kN.m per front wheel, 2-3 times a loaded wheel's grip), so a full pedal always runs in ABS. The brake force then follows each wheel's grip. When the outer front carries most of the load, it carries most of the braking, and braking the outer front is exactly what a stability control does to stop a car rotating.

The four questions

1. How modern splits the brakes

GT2's ABS law per wheel, every 1/120 s step: with GT2's oversized torques every wheel brakes at its own grip. There is no pedal balance, no proportioning and no select-low. Modern brakes 19 % harder than classic in this corner (end speed 139 against 146 km/h).

2. Is the load transfer physical

Yes, for a 0.43 m sprung CG (the per-car rule from GT2's body height). GT2 uses the ride height, 0.08 m here, as the arm: 535 N per g against 2,974. At 1.8 g lateral and 2.6 g braking the inner rear lifts. GT2's transfer in modern rotates less (-1.8°), not more.

3. Where the extra side grip comes from

It does not exist. An early measurement suggested it, but its first step after switching physics at 200 km/h carried 7 kN too much load. On a rollout, modern's side force in braking is 13 % below classic's (22.4 against 25.8 kN): more on the front, a third of classic's on the rear.

4. The share of each part

The outer front's brake force: 90-99 %. GT2's 30 Hz step: 22-33 % (an artifact). Real-size brake torque: 20-41 %. Rear select-low: 17-33 %. The tire's load sensitivity: 17-53 % (with more braking). GT2's transfer: -17 %. The shares overlap and do not add up.

What is not missing

From classic's own state on the throttle (85 km/h, -10° body slip) modern fishtails as classic does: +49.4° against +51.3° of heading in 1 s. On the 50 m circle both physics spin the stock 300ZX under a full brake, and modern rotates more on a lift-off (FR 300ZX yaw gain 1.62 against 1.24; MR MR2 1.59 against 1.43). The gap is specific to braking into a corner on a high-grip car, where the load moves onto the outer front while it is already braking at its grip.

The change: brakes at real size

Modern physics now sizes each car's brakes to the car and makes the ABS a ceiling that can only lower the pedal's torque. Classic is untouched; modern recordings made before the change still replay with GT2's brakes.

The rule

sizing state
a straight-line stop at the car's own limit at 200 km/h: wheel loads with the downforce and the longitudinal transfer, the tire's peak brake force
need per wheel
F r + J a / r: the tire's torque plus the rotating mass it slows (wheel, drive line, engine in first; 4WD axles share theirs)
capacity
T_cap = s T_GT2, one s = min(1, 1.2 max(need / T_GT2)) for both axles
ABS
T = min(p T_cap, T_abs): it only releases; GT2's law was p min(T, T_abs)

Why the digital brake gains little

A brake sized to reach a loaded wheel's grip in a straight line also reaches it on this corner's outer front: 4.5 kN.m of capacity against 4.5 kN.m of grip torque, so the outer front still brakes at 90 % of its grip. The blunt 1/3 scale tested earlier (3.4 kN.m) gave +4.6° because it was below this car's straight-line need, the same trap as the MR2. The rest of the gap is classic's 30 Hz ABS cycling, which modern does not copy.

With the stick the rotation is there: a partial pedal brakes both wheels of an axle alike, so the stabilizing moment goes away. Under GT2's law a partial pedal turned less (25.6° at 40 %).

The first pass, without the rotating mass in the need, left nine 4WD and FF kei cars on racing tires 14-21 % longer: their rear lifts and the front brakes also slow the rear's wheels, the drive line and the engine. 144 stock and 259 racing-tire cars keep GT2's torque, because it is already below 1.2 times their need.

The rule has two numbers: the margin 1.2 and the 200 km/h sizing speed. On modern the Brake Balance Controller is also a real front/rear balance, 5 % a step (GT2_MODERN_BRAKE_BALANCE=real; gt2 gives GT2's per-axle ABS gain).
