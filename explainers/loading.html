Inside GT2's Loading

What GT2 reads from the disc between choosing a race and the first frame on track, how long each part takes on a real console, where it all lands in the PS1's 2 MB of RAM, video memory and sound memory, and where the game's fixed six-car layout is built in.

Three loads, not one

  1. The overlay switch. GT2 does not keep all its code in memory: parts of it, called overlays, are loaded when needed. Going from GT Mode's code (ovl4) to the race code (ovl0): The whole overlay container GT2.OVL is read again and one member inflated, about 1.1 s on the drive, at every switch.
  2. The session's first drive: race strings, font and the effects bank, then engine sounds and car models (once per session), then the course (once per session).
  3. Start Race from the pre-race page: the course stays; the cars and engine sounds load again, now six cars.

Going back to the menus is another overlay switch plus GT Mode's own full reload: the car and race databases, the menu art, the 387 KB menu sound bank and the menu pages.

The timeline, on a console

Measured with a model of the console's 2x CD drive, one time scale for all three loads. On the console, the engine sounds cost more than all six car models: four sound banks of about 241 KB against six models and textures of about 110 KB compressed.

RAM during a race (2 MB)

Everything a race uses sits at a fixed offset from one race object. There is no free space that does not depend on the course: what a race leaves untouched is the tail of the course buffer, a second garage used only in two-player play, and each car slot past its model.

Drawn to scale by address, four rows of 512 KB. Hover for exact sizes. The primitive pool doubles as the load scratch: car textures, sky textures and the sponsor boards pass through it before the race starts.

Midfield's geometry is 450,412 B, so on that course 249,588 B of the buffer are never touched. The port's 8 MB mode moves the primitive pool (for a longer draw distance) and adds room for new files; the course and sky buffers, the six car slots and the race code stay where GT2 put them.

Video memory during a race

The PS1 has 1 MB of video memory, laid out as 1024 x 512 16-bit units. Frame buffers, the race font, the HUD, sponsor boards, course textures and one fixed slot per car share it.

Drawn to scale. Each car slot holds a 256 x 224 4-bit texture plus four light-variant palettes below it (car 0 at x 640, car 5 at x 320). Dashed areas are free in a race. Measured on Midfield: 258 free 16 x 16 cells, 132 KB of 1 MB, mostly in the two dashed areas and in holes of the course area.

Sound memory during a race (512 KB)

Every AI car sounds alike because one player's own engine set already takes about 160 KB, so all five opponents share one non-turbo and one turbo bank. A distinct engine per opponent would need about 140 KB each, which does not fit 512 KB with reverb. A two-player race (two player sets, no generic banks) leaves about 49 KB below the reverb area. On the way back to GT Mode, the 387,280-byte menu sound bank is loaded over the race effects and every engine bank, which is why the race reloads its effects at every entry.

This is a hard limit of the console's sound memory, not of the game logic.

Where the six-car assumptions sit

Six cars is not one constant. It is built into several layouts at once, and more cars in a race would need every one of them to grow.

PlaceWhat is fixed
race set-up6 entries, written by GT Mode before the race overlay starts

Nothing is shared between cars in RAM or video memory. Each texture passes through the one scratch buffer, one car at a time. Two entries with the same car load it twice. Per car: model 20,480 B inflated, texture exactly 45,984 B (16 paints at most), 192 wheels in the wheel table (all used), 305 of 328 engine sets.

Bugs and quirks

L1 · Sky buffer unchecked

CAUSE FOUND

Nothing checks the sky model's size. A sky over 22,528 B (a mod) would silently overwrite the course header that was loaded before it.

L2 · GT2.OVL read whole every switch

MEASURED

141 sectors read to inflate one member. The code has a cache this disc never sets. About 2.8 s of every GT Mode, race, menus round trip on a console.

L3 · Race database inflated twice

MEASURED

170,566 B inflated twice per GT Mode entry, about 0.16 s on the drive. Harmless.

L4 · A 46 KB buffer on the stack

CAUSE FOUND

The race overlay reads all languages' race strings into a 46,072-byte stack buffer and keeps 5,759 bytes. Safe only because the call is shallow.

L5 · Load speed changes the field

MEASURED

Opponents are drawn at random at Start Race, and the draw follows load timing: the same presses fielded different cars with fast loads and with the drive model.

P7 · Car texture errors are data

MEASURED

The G20 race car is a byte copy of the Primera's, the 3000GT race car lacks two logos, and two pairs of Golfs share textures. The loader picks the night files by a course flag, so night-only issues show only on night courses.

No loading hang, buffer overrun or wrong file was seen in any run.
