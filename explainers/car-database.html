Every car is a list of part numbers

A Gran Turismo 2 car is one 72-byte record that points at 27 part records. When a race starts, the game turns those parts and your settings into a 448-byte race spec, the block of numbers the physics reads. This page shows how that works.

Three files, one format

All three car databases are GTDT files: a short index of blocks, each block an array of fixed-size records. The record sizes are not in the file; they are a table inside the game's program, one per database. The first 30 blocks are the same part kinds everywhere (brakes, engines, gearboxes, tires...), so a car in any mode is built the same way.

Opponents in GT mode events come from a fourth file, usa_gtmode_race.dat: 1,220 opponent records naming 451 different cars, each with its own settings and a power multiplier. Colors and the region filter (26 cars hidden in the US game) live in .carinfoa, not in the database.

From parts to physics

Your tune is the 0x84-byte set-up (132 bytes): part indices plus every setting. The hex numbers in the boxes below are sizes in bytes (0x84 = 132, 0xC4 = 196, 0x1C0 = 448). At race start the game expands the set-up step by step.

The sports gearboxes have no ratios on file

All three shop transmissions (Sports, Semi-Racing, Full-Racing) store 0xFFFF where the ratios should be. The game computes them from one number, the auto level: the top gear reaches the rev limit at auto level x 10 km/h on the driven wheel, each lower gear is a step shorter, the steps grow down the box, and reverse is three quarters of first plus a quarter of second. It does this when the box is fitted, when the car enters the garage, and at race start for opponents.

Same numbers as the game

The port builds cars the same way the original does. For every one of the 618 cars (stock, garage set-up, full tune, every setting at its minimum and maximum), for every part each car can fit (21,747 combinations), and for every event opponent, arcade car and license car, the race spec comes out identical to the game's own.

What the setting numbers really mean

The settings screens show plain numbers. Here is what each stored byte stands for in real units.

SettingWhat the byte means
Springsa position 10..255 mapped onto 1.6..8.5 kgf/mm: 30 is 2.1 kgf/mm, not 3.0
Damperstwo forces per direction: at 0.3 m/s and 0.7 m/s, 49 N per unit
Stabilizersquadratic anti-roll, 98 N per m² of left-right stroke difference
Brakes98 N·m per unit; the third brake byte is the handbrake
Brake balance controlleran ABS gain per axle; it never moves torque front to rear
AWDdrivetrain byte +7 = front torque share %; five center modes, one forced on dirt
LSD kindan ASCII letter: f none, r locked, n open, m and h LSD, v viscous, y yaw control, t torque transfer

A detail worth knowing: shop items 44 and 45 are "Simulation Tires" (grip byte 115) and "Dirt Tires" (grip byte 200), going by the game's own name strings. They are easy to mix up, and a mix-up makes the dirt tire look worse on dirt than it is.

Addresses are for the US Simulation disc (SCUS-94488). No game data beyond a few quoted numbers.
