GT2 port · physics · tune parts on classic and modern

What each tune does, classic and modern

Every tune part GT2 sells, tested on both of the port's physics: every part at every stage, every setting at both ends of its range, and the full tune, on 11 cars. Classic is GT2's own physics at 30 updates a second; modern is the port's rewrite in real-world units at 120 steps a second. The question: does a tune do more on modern, and by how much, part by part? The test runs only press the controller buttons; nothing about the cars or the physics was changed for them.

The short answer

On these tests, modern does not give tunes more weight overall. Across every part and setting on the metrics it acts on, the median ratio of modern's effect to classic's is 0.99 (1495 cells); all of modern's effects summed against all of classic's: 0.97.

"Ratio" is modern's effect divided by classic's: 1.00 means a part does the same on both. "Weight" adds up the size of all effects on each side and divides them, which stays meaningful when an effect is near zero.

same
Power and drivetrain parts, gearing, full tune. Racing Chip, Port Polishing, Engine Balancing, NA Tune, Turbo, Intercooler, Muffler, Clutch, Flywheel, Carbon Drive Shaft, Gear Box, Weight Reduction, Final Gear and Auto Setting move 0-100, 400 m and top speed by the same amount on both (ratio 1.00). Full tune, classic / modern effect: 0-100 -2.50 / -2.57 s (ratio 1.00, weight 1.00); braking -6.62 / -6.71 m (ratio 1.02, weight 1.11); skidpad +0.233 / +0.251 g (ratio 0.96, weight 0.96); Autumn Ring -12.17 / -11.69 s (ratio 0.95, weight 0.96).
close
Tires. Sports and Racing tires: skidpad +0.119 / +0.117 g (ratio 0.91, weight 0.84); braking -3.96 / -3.84 m (ratio 0.99, weight 0.99); lap -3.25 / -3.47 s (ratio 1.04, weight 1.03). Dirt Tires gain a little less on modern: tarmac +0.283 / +0.278 g (ratio 0.85, weight 0.83), dirt +0.334 / +0.244 g (ratio 0.72, weight 0.75).
smaller
Suspension kits and the LSD. Suspension on the skidpad +0.044 / +0.022 g (ratio 0.32, weight 0.82), on the lap -1.71 / -1.34 s (ratio 0.69, weight 0.83). The LSD changes classic's steady circle on some cars (the MR2 loses 0.08 g with any LSD); modern's LSD hardly does.
noise
Most settings. Spring Rate, Ride Height, Shock Absorber, Toe, Stabilizer, the LSD settings and Downforce move the steady skidpad by less than the 0.024 g noise on both. Camber is the exception and acts the same on both: skidpad -0.028 / -0.029 g (ratio 1.00, weight 1.09), braking +0.59 / +0.69 m (ratio 1.01, weight 1.12).
none
Sports Brakes, Brake Balance Controller, ASC, TCS. The stops are grip-limited (the ABS never asks for more torque than the tire passes), as in measurements of the original game; ASC and TCS stay inactive in straight runs and steady circles on both.

Where modern turns a tune around

Heavy FR cars' braking: full tune, Brake Balance front max

Supra RZ full tune: classic stops 4.4 m longer, modern 5.9 m shorter. Brake Balance front max: classic +4.0 m (Supra) and +3.2 m (Mustang), modern about 0.

GT2's ABS sets each wheel's brake once per 30 Hz tick. Above a loop gain of about 3 the loaded front wheels lock and release in turn. A full tune raises that gain (more grip, a lighter drive line). A front-heavy balance, by the ABS formula's form (not traced here), pushes the fronts into that regime. Modern's ABS is not tick-bound. So this is a quirk of classic; modern behaves like a real car.

Weight Reduction on braking

Mustang Stage 3: classic -0.6 m, modern +0.7 m; the 4WD cars by similar amounts.

Traced on the Lancer, Stage 1: both strategies decelerate 1.5-2 % less at speed after the reduction (100-70 km/h 1.316 to 1.292 g classic, 1.327 to 1.307 g modern). Classic's stock car is weak in the last 10 km/h (0.77 g), and the lighter car less so (1.04 g); that low-speed end flips classic's total. Not a modern effect.

Gearing on 4WD laps

R34 GT-R, Final Gear max: classic -0.4 s, modern +0.2 s on Autumn Ring; the auto setting the same way; the Lancer likewise.

Small (under 1 s on a 90 s lap); the straight-line effects agree (0-100 ratio 1.00). Modern's 4WD center coupling was checked and is not the cause. What remains is how the classic AI's lap uses the gears on a 4WD car. Not traced.

Final Gear min on the RX-7's launch; extreme camber on the kei car's lap

RX-7, Final Gear 2.500: 0-100 classic -0.13 s, modern +0.20 s. Honda Z, Camber front 12.0 deg: lap classic +2.1 s, modern -0.5 s.

Not traced. Probable cause for the RX-7: modern's lower launch-slip engine speed bogs a tall first gear. For the Honda Z, skidpad and braking agree (both lose grip with that camber); the lap is one AI lap on a 62 PS car.

How it was measured

  • Cars (11): RX-7 GT-C a2gcn, Supra RZ t2ssn, Skyline GT-R R34 n24rn (4WD code 5: GT2's drivetrain type number), Mustang GT ufm9n, Vitz F t2vzn, MR2 G-Limited t2mgn, Civic Type R h2crn, Castrol Mugen NSX GT h2cnr (race car), Lancer Evolution VI m2g6n (code 3), Impreza WRX STi Ver.VI s2i6n (code 4), Honda Z Turbo h2ztn (code 2).
  • Changes: every part at every stage against stock; every setting at both ends of its range against the part that makes it adjustable at its defaults; the full tune against stock. Each change is made through the game's own settings screens.
  • Straight: full throttle on an endless flat straight: 0-100, 0-200 km/h, 400 m, top speed. Braking: 100-0 km/h from 120 km/h.
  • Skidpad: the highest steady speed held before the first loss, as centripetal g, on five circles (40, 50, 60, 70, 80 m), averaged. Two radii were too noisy for settings: one change's effect differed by 0.04 g between 50 and 60 m at the median. The noise floor is the median standard error over the five radii, 0.024 g.
  • Lap: Autumn Ring Free Run, GT2's AI driving on both physics, the flying lap. Dirt (tire rows): 0-100, braking and the 50 m skidpad on an all-dirt plane.
  • Ratio = modern effect / classic effect, only where classic's effect passes the metric's floor (2 ticks or 1 % for 0-100, 0.5 km/h for top speed, 0.3 m or 1 % for braking, 0.1 s for the lap, 0.024 g for the skidpad).
