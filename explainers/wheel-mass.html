The wheels' mass and the wheel-tire coupling: M3 and M1

GT2 takes two shortcuts in how the wheels take part in the car's acceleration: M3 (every loaded wheel's equivalent mass is added to the body) and M1 (the wheel's spin feels only part of the tire's force at low speed). Modern physics first kept both. This page measures each one alone and both together, and asks what modern should use on physical grounds only (lap times, 0-100 times and license gold counts are reported, not used as a reason). Classic physics is GT2 exactly and keeps both shortcuts.

Result: the port's modern physics now uses the real-world form of both on driven wheels (the "physical pair", v3 below). On braked wheels GT2's extra mass is still kept, for the reason in section 5. Offsets like wheel+0x63 are byte positions inside the game's record for one wheel in memory.

1. What GT2 does, and why each is a shortcut

M3: the wheels' mass in the body

  • GT2: the longitudinal acceleration is (F_drive + F_drag + F_roll) / (m + sum I_axle / r^2): car+0x43C holds 4 I_w / r^2 per axle (kg x 4), summed over the loaded wheels into the inverse mass, whether the wheel is driven, braked or rolling free. The code's own mask says what was meant: it sums the wheels with (wheel+0x63 & 13) == 0, which skips an unloaded wheel (bit 0) and the driven wheels (bits 2 and 3, set by the drivetrain step), but the flags are rebuilt earlier in the same tick (wheel+0x63 = (T0 != 0) << 1 | (load == 0), in the brake step) and the driven bits are only ORed in after the sum, so every loaded wheel counts. Bit 1 (braked) is not in the mask: braked wheels were meant to count. GT2's wheel inertias are about 5x a real wheel's (5-8.5 kg.m^2), so each wheel adds 75-95 kg.
  • Modern keeps the term (m_long = m + sum_loaded s_w I_w / r^2) and integrates the wheels' spins on their own: a driven or braked wheel by its implicit spin step I dw/dt = tau - r F (tau the drive and brake torque), a free (undriven, unbraked) loaded wheel rolls at the ground speed (as in GT2) and passes no force.
  • The physics. A wheel whose spin has its own equation resists acceleration through its tire force: F = tau / r - I a / r^2 when it rolls with the car (a = r dw/dt). Its inertia therefore already reaches the body through F, and adding I / r^2 to the body counts it a second time. A wheel without its own equation (modern's free wheel, held at the ground speed) has no other way to make the body pay for spinning it up, so for it I / r^2 in the body is exactly right (m_eff = m + I / r^2 is the textbook rolling-wheel mass). Physical: s_w = 1 on free wheels, 0 on driven and on braked wheels. GT2 has 1 everywhere: about +150-190 kg on a 2WD car accelerating (x2 on a 4WD), +300-380 kg on any car braking with all four wheels.

M1: the wheel feels part of the tire's force

  • GT2: the tire's force f = grip x A(slip) goes to the body whole (wheel+0x58 / +0x5C) and to the wheel's spin as k x f, with k = wheel+0x2A = min(4096, 2048 + |v_patch| x 147 / 8192) (in 1/4096 units, where 4096 = 1): k = 0.5 + 0.01794 |v|, 0.5 at rest, 1 from 100.3 km/h. The same k also scales the slip ratio .
  • The physics. The force between road and tire acts on both, equal and opposite (Newton's third law at the patch): m a = sum F on the body, I dw/dt = tau - r F on the wheel. With GT2's k F on the wheel the engine pays k F (w r) for a body that receives F v: below 100 km/h (1 - k) F v of the body's energy has no source (the energy accounting's residual, 15-27 % of the engine's work in a stock 0-100 km/h). Braking mirrors it: the wheel feels only k of the tire's braking force, so the brake torque overcomes it sooner and the wheel locks earlier and harder than the tire's force would let it. Physical: k = 1 on the wheel.
  • What M1's physical form does not change: the k in the slip ratio (it stretches the slip axis of GT2's tire curve at low speed: a softer tire below 100 km/h; it moves no momentum or energy between bodies, so it is a tire model property, not a conservation question), the 20 km/h slip fade, and the ABS loop gain k x 12 (a controller).

Why M1 and M3 should change together

The two shortcuts push the acceleration in opposite directions: M3 adds mass (slower), M1 makes energy (quicker). GT2 was tuned with both, so their sum is close to a real car's acceleration, but each is wrong on its own. Section 3 measures each one alone and both together: an energy accounting (the "ledger") shows which combination conserves energy.

  • M1 is the energy leak. With GT2's coupling 18 % of the work that reaches the body has no source (every car, stock or tuned); with one force on wheel and body the ledger closes to within 1 % (the rest is the integration and the contacts), with or without M3. M3 does not move the residual: a double-counted mass is a wrong mass, not a wrong energy flow (its energy is stored and given back).

(The skidpad moves +-8 % per car between any two variants, M3 alone included, though its median stays within 0.4 %: the skidpad search's per-car spread, not a grip change; top speed does not move: both terms act only while the speed changes.)

  • M3 alone (v1) takes 9 % off every 0-100 (each drivetrain group 9-12 %, 4WD code 2 17 %) and keeps the order (rho 0.998): a uniform mass.
  • M1 alone (v2) adds 16 % to the 0-100 and moves the order (rho 0.985 stock, 0.929 full): the energy GT2's coupling made was worth most at the launch, and it was not the same for every layout (group medians: FF +17 %, 4WD +11-18 %, FR +5 %, RR -1 %).
  • The pair (v3) lands at +3 % on the 0-100 and -7 % on the 0-200 (above 100 km/h only M3 acts), with a layout pattern: FF +5.9 %, 4WD codes 3-5 +1-5 %, MR +0.9 %, FR -3.3 %, RR -8.0 %, 4WD code 2 -10 % (group medians against classic). That is the physical pattern of a launch: under acceleration the load moves rearward, so a rear-driven car gets more traction and a front-driven one less; GT2's coupling hid it by letting every driven wheel spin up as if it felt half its force. The largest 0-100 rank moves are three high-power cars that get about 30 % slower (Wiegert Vector W8 and Vector M12, MR, 6.5 -> 8.5 s, rank 212 -> 365-369 of 618; Corvette ZR-1 '95, FR, 6.2 -> 7.6 s): the ledger shows a longer launch (the W8: clutch slip 74 -> 90 kJ, the engine's mean power 124 -> 105 kW over the run); why the launch bogs is not traced.
  • Braked wheels (v4 against v3) only change the stop: 100-0 km/h -19 % (p10 .. p90 -28 .. -14 %). The stock median stop is then 25.2 m, a mean deceleration of 1.56 g against the same cars' 1.26 g on the skidpad (full tunes: 1.88 g against 1.53 g). With GT2's M3 on the braked wheels the stop is 1.26 g (1.60 g full), the skidpad's grip: the braked wheels' second mass (about 25 % of a road car's) is what holds GT2's stop at its lateral grip, because GT2's longitudinal tire curve peaks about a quarter above its lateral one. Counting the braked wheels once is the right form, but alone it exposes that tire peak: road tires stop at about their lateral grip (the longitudinal peak of a real tire is within about 10 % of its lateral peak).

The answer to "does the driven-wheel mass change rotation with an LSD?": not the LSD's own action; a little through the acceleration. The LSD's clutch acts between the two driven wheels' spin steps, which M3 does not enter: the inside driven wheel's peak slip changes by at most 0.002 with every LSD at every M3, and each LSD's lift-off effect keeps its sign and size within the circle maneuvers' noise (about +-0.02: the 1-way +0.08 .. +0.105, the 2-way -0.067 .. -0.099). M3 is a mass in the body's equation: at 0 the same drive force accelerates the car 6.6 % harder (power-on 0.274 -> 0.292 g), more load moves rearward, and a lift-off decelerates harder (the stock car's tuck-in +0.217 -> +0.231 at 0.5-1 s, +0.448 -> +0.476 at 1-2 s). The stock car's power-on push stays within the noise (-0.198 .. -0.238); the LSDs' small power-on effect at 50 m (+0.02-0.03) is at the noise floor at every M3. M1's physical form moves the lift-off more than M3 does (stock +0.217 -> +0.189; the 2-way's calming -0.086 -> -0.114): with the wheel feeling the whole tire force, the engine's braking slows the driven wheels less sharply on a lift.

4. Should M1 change together with M3?

Yes, on physical grounds, and the measurements say why:

  • 1. Each is a separate error with a separate test. M1 breaks the action-reaction pair at the patch: the ledger shows it as 18 % of the engine's work made from nothing, with or without M3 (3.1). M3 counts one inertia twice: it shows as a mass, not as an energy error. Fixing M3 alone (v1) leaves the energy leak and makes every car 9 % quicker on a mass that was never there; fixing M1 alone (v2) closes the ledger but keeps a body 150-190 kg too heavy while accelerating (2WD), so the 0-100 is 16 % slower. Neither alone is physics.
  • 2. Together they are the conservation laws: one force on wheel and body (momentum at the patch) and each wheel's inertia once (the free wheels in the body, the driven and braked wheels in their spin). The ledger then closes to 0.5 % (stock) / 0.8 % (full).
  • 3. GT2's two shortcuts did not cancel, they only roughly offset in the median: the pair is +3 % (stock) / +4 % (full) on the 0-100, with a layout pattern (FF slower, RR and FR quicker) that is the physical one. So switching only one of them is the configuration to avoid.
  • 4. What stays a separate question: the k in the slip ratio (a low-speed tire-curve stretch, not a conservation question), GT2's wheel inertias (5x a real wheel), and the engine inertia reaching the wheels by the gear ratio instead of its square. None of these was changed or measured here.

5. What modern uses

The physical pair: M1 physical and no extra mass on driven wheels (v3). Both are conservation laws (section 4); together the energy ledger closes to under 1 %, and the resulting launch pattern (rear-driven cars gain, front-driven ones lose) is the physical one. Known limit: GT2's launch logic (the clutch slip phase and the launch rpm, tuned with M1's half-force wheels) bogs on a few high-power cars with the pair: the Vector W8 and Vector M12 (0-100 6.5 -> 8.5 s) and the Corvette ZR-1 '95. That is the launch logic's behavior with a correct wheel, and it is not fixed.

Braked wheels keep GT2's extra mass. Counting the braked wheels once is the right form, but on its own it makes road cars stop at 1.56 g (fully tuned 1.88 g), a quarter above their own lateral grip (1.26 / 1.53 g), which road tires do not do. GT2's braking distance comes from the double count holding a too-high longitudinal tire peak down. Changing the mass and that tire peak together is one change; the mass alone would trade one non-physical result for another.

What the pair changes against modern's first version (stock medians): 0-100 +3 %, 0-200 -7 %, lap -0.6 %, braking +0.9 %; the 0-100 rank order stays close (rho 0.9868). Classic is unchanged.
