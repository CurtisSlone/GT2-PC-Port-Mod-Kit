GT2 Race Flow

How Gran Turismo 2 runs a race from the pre-race page to the prize money: the steps around a race, a single frame, the countdown, lap timing, positions, the finish and the results.

Two machines, one inside the other

A "state machine" is a list of steps plus rules for which step comes next. GT2 uses two here. A session runs the pages around a race and the drives. A drive is the race itself, run once per frame until it says stop. The session decides what comes after a drive from one byte: how the drive ended.

The GT Mode session. Steps S1, S3, S7 and S9 only pass control on and are left out of the drawing. In a race (kind 2), Start Race on the pre-race page rebuilds the session and goes straight to the drive.

One drive, start to finish

Inside a drive the race moves through four phases. The moment the player crosses the finish line is the moment GT2 writes the result.

A Test Run has no hold (rolling start): GO comes on the first frame. You can pause any time before you finish; Exit ends the drive.

Inside one frame

GT2 races at 30 frames a second, one physics tick per frame (a "tick" is one step of the simulation). The order below is fixed, and it matters: lap lines see the positions of the tick before, and the on-screen timers run after the camera.

one race frame  (every 2 VBlanks = 1/30 s)
  pads · pause check · race hold −2 fields
  ┌ physics tick ──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ A controls + AI driver · B collisions (not during the hold) │
  │ C–F wheels, road probe, suspension                          │
  │ G progress: each car in running order → lap lines, wrong way│
  │ H race clock +100 (1/3000 s)                                │
  │ J positions (insertion sort)   K rubber band                │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
  camera · HUD timers · countdown · finish sequence (0 = stop)
  exit code (1 replay over, 2 Exit) · music · car sounds

A VBlank is one 60 Hz screen refresh; a "field" is the same 1/60 s unit.

The rules, as GT2 computes them

A line crossed inside a tick

A car rarely sits exactly on the line at a tick, so the crossing time is worked out by distance inside the 1/30 s tick, in hundredths of the tick, then cut down to the millisecond.

frac = 100 × (L − d0) / (L − d0 + d1)
t_ms = (clock + frac) / 3
lap  = t_ms − last line time
L = course length · d0, d1 = distance before and after

Positions

Laps first, then distance along the course. A car that has finished is frozen in the order once ranked: nobody passes it, it passes nobody.

key  = lap − (crossed the line backward ? 1 : 0)
sort = stable insertion, every tick

The finish

Each car finishes when its own lap counter reaches the race's laps, then drives a cool-down lap (or stops: rally, point-to-point, license test). Laps after that are ignored.

Points are taken when you finish

At the player's finish every car gets points for the place it holds right then. AI cars still racing keep the place they had.

Prize money

After the replay, the result page pays the event's prize for your place. Credits stop at 99,999,999. A single-race win adds one prize car, picked at random.

Random numbers

No shared random state. Each caller steps its own seed, taken from the VBlank count. A race draws its music at the start, the trackside boards at the load, the prize car at the end, and nothing in between. More on the random numbers page.

s = 17·s + 17
r = s XOR rotate16(s)

Countdown and finish sequence

The countdown runs 240 fields past the hold: beeps at 3, 2 and 1 s, GO at 0. After the player's finish a frame counter runs to 316 (single race) or 569 (championship), waiting for a press at its stop frames.

Championships and the 2-hour limit

Standings sort by total points, with ties going to the player. A 99-lap race ends at each car's first lap line after two hours of race clock.

Where GT2 assumes six cars

Six cars on track is not one setting you can change: it is built into many parts of the game.
