GT2 Simulation (US) · the car settings screens

Every setting is a byte, every range is a part

Gran Turismo 2's settings screens edit a work copy of your car. Each setting is one or two bytes of the 132-byte set-up (the record that holds your car's parts and tune); its range comes from the part you fitted; one press moves it one raw unit; leaving the screen writes it back to the garage. These rules were checked against the game for every car.

Two screens over one work copy

On the pre-race page, Settings ... opens CHANGE PARTS (fit a part you own); L1 switches to PARTS SETTING (adjust), R1 back. Both work on the tune sheet: the set-up plus every part record your car could fit, built when you entered the race. Nothing reaches the garage until you leave. In the drawing, +0x numbers are byte offsets inside a record and 0x8016E584 is the tune sheet's address in the PlayStation's RAM.

Inside the edit box, Cross or Circle writes the values into the sheet and Triangle or Square throws them away. There is no "cancel all": once you leave, the sheet is the car.

Fitting a part resets that part's settings to the new part's defaults (suspension, brake controller, gearbox, turbo, NA tune, racing modification, ASC, TCS, LSD); everything else is kept.

The custom gearbox: ranges from a computed box

The custom gearbox stores no ratios. When it is fitted, or when you change the auto setting, the game computes a box from the target speed (the auto setting x 10 km/h), your final drive and your tires. Each gear may then move a third of the way toward its neighbors; first gear up to 12.5 % shorter, top gear up to 12.5 % taller. The ranges never overlap, so the gears keep their order.

RX-7 GT-C (a2gcn), custom box just fitted, auto setting 24 (240 km/h), final 4.300 (range 2.500..5.500). Bars: the allowed range; dot: the computed ratio.

The ranges and Start on the ratios come from the box computed at the last fit or auto setting change. Change your tires afterwards and they still describe the old box until you touch the auto setting again.

Try it: the TCS slider

The screen lets the TCS byte go from 0 to 50 (the kit's min..max). The race spec spreads it over the kit's 50 steps starting at 1, so 1 is off, 50 is full, and 0 falls below the start of the table.

Redesigned screens, same rules

The port's settings screens can look different from GT2's, but what they do to the set-up stays the same: locked or out-of-range edits are refused, Default and Fit have exactly the effects listed above (gear ratios refilled, a part's settings reset, NA tune and turbo kit removing each other), and the raw values stay what the race reads.

Offsets and addresses are for the US Simulation disc (SCUS-94488). The gearbox example is computed from the game's own car data.
