GT2 Race Presentation

The parts of a race you see and hear around the cars: tire smoke and the remaster look's extra particles, the sponsor boards GT2 picks at random, GT2's object level of detail, the results page and license medal page at the finish, and the menu music. All of it is presentation: it reads the race and never changes it, so turning any of it on or off cannot change a race result.

Particles beside the world

In a race GT2 draws one kind of particle: the tire smoke puffs from its smoke pool. There are no sparks or debris in GT2. UGT2R draws the puffs in GT2's two images, read from your disc at load (the 32 × 32 gray smoke and the 48 × 48 dust), blended the way GT2 blends them (additive: they brighten what is behind). The remaster look adds flying dirt, dust and sparks, made once per tick from the race state.

Sponsor boards

GT2 picks the trackside boards at random when the course loads and draws them into fixed slots of video memory. Each course has a board table: 90 boards in 5 sets, 10 named sets of per-board chances, and 5 default images. The port runs GT2's own pick, so you see the same boards GT2 would show for the same seed.

The pick

  • Set: the race set-up's board set in a GT Mode race, else the race code's default. A course with .crsinfo flag 0x40 gets none.
  • Seed: the race set-up's board seed (taken from the VBlank count, see random numbers).
  • Per set: a shuffle and one 12-bit draw per board; board k goes j-th into slot order[j], its color table to the set's row plus the slot. Slots left over get the set's default image.

Why it matters

Without the pick, a course shows only the default boards stored in its file. On one General01 race (seed 0x7733), the board area is 38 rectangles, 15,088 words of video memory. The course file alone gets 2,040 of those words right; GT2's pick gets all of them.

GT2's object level of detail

"Level of detail" (LOD) means drawing a simpler version of an object when it is far away. The port follows GT2's rules for this:

  • Each object switches to its less detailed level past GT2's LOD distance, and only one level of a model is drawn at a time.
  • The course is split into sections; groups of objects the camera's section leaves out are not drawn.
  • Some objects fade from one level to the other instead of switching at once. Trees and rocks get extra depth and detail in the remaster look's sharper picture only.
  • The rear-view mirror has its own section, so its view of the track is not cut by the main camera's.

The finish: results page and medal page

GT2 draws a results page over the race's last pictures during the finish sequence, timed by the sequence's two frame counters. UGT2R draws it the same way for every race kind that shows it, the battle and a championship's points pages. License tests get the medal page from the same step's license part.

The medal rule

Bronze, silver and gold need a time under their targets (strictly under). The "kids" prize appears only on the near miss that reaches the test's limit with no result yet; otherwise the page shows "FAIL" with the time. More on the license tests page.

The dirt-course license demos

Six demonstrations (S-3, S-8, IA-4, IA-6, IB-1, IB-2) only finish at their stored times when driven with the car spec stored in the demo file, which carries an older dirt tire. Built from the car database instead, those six crash or never finish. With the stored spec, all 23 demos checked finish at the times GT2 shows.

Menu music: a sequencer, not a stream

GT2's menu music is sequenced, like MIDI: songs (SEQ files) played note by note over a bank of instrument samples by the game's sound driver. The race music is different: streamed XA audio from the disc. The port plays the menu songs the same way, from the files on your disc.

Small facts

  • The city map tune loops after 57 s.
  • While a menu page loads, GT2 skips its music step for a few fields (6 in one measured session), and the music waits.
  • GT2 has a fade-out with a quirk (it stops short by a remainder), but the game never starts one.

The port's timing for the hub and Go Race tunes was compared note for note with GT2 (over 10,000 notes, all the same). The other songs run on the same player.
