The Showroom Car, Native

Can the dealer, garage and shop cars in GT Mode be drawn by the same modern car renderer the port's remaster look uses in races, with the upscaled body (about 5,400 triangles) and the upscaled paints? Yes: the menu car is GT2's race car drawing with the most detailed model forced, and that model is exactly the one the upscaled body is made from. What differs is the room around the car, not the car. This page lists what the race already has, what the menu adds, and how the game itself stays untouched. It is a study; the menus still show GT2's own car today.

Now built. The port draws the dealer, garage and shop cars this way by default (--scene-menu-car on; off gives GT2's own drawing, guide 13). This page is the study it was built from; a few details were settled while building it: the menu car keeps GT2's own studio reflection map, softened, and no live reflections.

One car, two rooms

The port converts each car once into its own scene format. The race and the menu would read the same converted car; only the frame around the car changes.

The menu always loads the day model, so it uses the day version of each car. Solid: built and in every race. Dashed: what the port adds for the menus.

The menu's own reflection map

A studio, not a sky

The menu car's shine reads texture page 9 with the palette at (576, 152). In GT Mode that page holds gt_cursor.tim, the sheet with the hand cursor and the badges. Its top-left 128 x 128 texels are a dark sphere map (a round picture used as a fake reflection) with two soft studio lights. The race cars reflect a blue sky and a tree line instead (crstim.arc element 3).

Memory and time stay GT2's

In the menus, GT2 even stores how long its own drawing took. So the plan leaves the game completely alone: GT2 still runs its camera, car and floor drawing as before, and the port only reads what it needs (the camera, which car, its paint and dirt). When the picture is put together, the port draws its own car into the item's rectangle in place of GT2's polygons. If the port's car is not ready yet, GT2's car is shown as today. The game's memory and timing come out identical; only the picture changes.

What the menu needs that the race does not

Camera and light

  • Camera: GT2's own, read after it is set up; the viewport is the item's rectangle.
  • Light: none in GT2: the paint as stored. The port does the same.
  • Turntable lights: come from the reflection, which follows the camera's heading: the highlights slide over the body as the camera orbits, as in GT2.

Floor, fade, wheels, paints

  • Floor: the port's own disc under a soft shadow; over GT2's floor a soft shadow would show as a dark patch.
  • Fade-in: GT2 has none. Proposed: load ahead, and a short cross-fade only when the port's car arrives late.
  • Wheel sizes: the menu's narrower track and garage tires are already known.
  • Paint chips: load all of a dealer car's paints when it loads, so changing paint never stutters.

How it was checked

  • The camera the port reads equals GT2's every frame; the turn steps by 12 a frame.
  • Original and port at the PS1 look: the same car outline on screen (within 1 pixel) and the floor circle in the same place, over 30 frames.
  • The same paint colors, and the studio highlights in the same places.
  • Side-by-side sheets: original, port at the PS1 look, port at the remaster look, and the same car in a race, for several cars on the dealer, garage, wheel shop, wash and car info pages.
