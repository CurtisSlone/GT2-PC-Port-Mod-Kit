Inside GT2's Cameras and Replays

How GT2 places the camera behind your car, how the TV cameras of a replay pick their shots, and how a replay is stored. The short version: a GT2 replay holds no positions at all. It keeps your controller inputs and plays the whole race again, so it only works if the simulation runs exactly as it did the first time.

The player cameras

The view button (R1 on the default set-up) steps through three cameras. Holding L1 turns either one 180° to look back. All three share one field of view, set in the options.

Side view, drawn to scale (meters). The chase cameras start at the car's position, turn to its heading and pitch minus the lag, then move 1.8 m up and back along their own axes. They follow the car's pitch, so they dip with it over a crest.

CameraPositionSmoothing
in-carthe car's own render matrix, 0.8 m upnone
chase near1.8 m up, 5.4 m back (split screen: 1.26 m, 6.48 m)lag spring on yaw and pitch
chase far1.8 m up, 6.8 m back (split screen: 1.62 m, 8.5 m)lag spring on yaw and pitch
mirrora copy of the in-car camera turned backwardnone

Field of view: one setting for all three, 60°, 73° or 80° wide. No camera tests walls or the ground, so the chase camera can sit inside a wall or under a crest. The cameras run once per tick, right after that tick's physics, and read the car's pose from it, so no camera lags its car by a tick.

The lag spring is part of the physics

The chase camera's swing is a spring-damper on yaw and pitch. Its state lives in each car's physics record and is updated in the physics tick for every car, not by the camera code. The camera only reads the result.

How much of a sudden heading change the camera still trails, over time. Computed from the constants in real units, not measured in a race: the game itself runs integer steps at 30 Hz. Option 0 still trails 20% at 0.5 s; option 1 trails 4%.

  • Option 0 is slightly under-damped and follows the car's heading.
  • Option 1 is stiffer and, above a speed threshold, aims along the direction of travel instead: a sliding car is seen from behind its path.
  • Bug C1: every car's spring uses the time step the last car left behind (car 5 in a race), including that AI car's rubber-band time scale. Your chase camera follows with an AI car's clock. With one car on track it is right. The port's classic physics keeps it.

The start fly-by

While the countdown runs, the camera does three moves: it flies in from ahead of the car, orbits it, then zooms out into your chosen camera. Its length is the start jingle's, 9 s in every race measured, so the phases stretch with the countdown rather than being a fixed 180 fields. The renderer has to follow it: during the fly-by the picture center is shifted by 80 lines.

TV cameras: a table in every course

Each course file carries a list of camera records keyed by lap and track distance, up to about 90 on a circuit. Each tick the replay takes the first record whose lap range and distance range contain the watched car. Later records that also match are never used.

The replay pad

  • Circle: TV, your cameras, or the other car
  • Square: 9 player views (in-car, chase near and far, overhead, 5 on-board)
  • Up / Down: the car watched, by race position
  • Start pauses; Select hides the HUD

There is no fast forward, slow motion or rewind in the replay code.

What a replay stores

GT2 records only the player car's controller input, once per physics tick, after the controller set-up has mapped it. With it go the race set-up (course, cars, entries, laps), the player's car spec and a 252-byte timing block. Not stored: positions, any car state, the random state.

ByteThe per-tick input record
0flags: analog steering, analog throttle, analog brake, analog curve
1logical buttons: left, right, throttle, brake, two more, shift up, shift down
2steering, 0 to 255 (128 = center)
3throttle step, 0 to 15
4brake step, 0 to 15 (stored with byte 3 in one byte)

Because the stream holds what the game read after the controller mapping, a replay plays the same on any controller set-up. The attract demos, the license demos and the rally ghosts are the same kind of stream. A rally ghost is a developer's lap installed as a second car with no contacts.

Why it is exact, and why old replays need classic physics

GT2's simulation is deterministic: the same inputs from the same start give the same race, tick for tick. Three checks show the replayed race is the driven one.

CheckResult
License B-1 driven, then its Replaythe car's position, speed, heading and every physics field identical tick for tick over 1,503 ticks
Attract demo MSC0002 (Seattle, 6 cars, 7,147 ticks)the player's 8 lap and split times stored in the replay (1:57.422 / 1:46.879 and 6 splits) are hit exactly
License demo IB-4 with IB-4's own rulesfinishes in 24.258 s, the developer's stored time, PASS
The same IB-4 demo under B-1's rulesleaves the road after 13.4 s: a standing start instead of IB-4's 150 km/h rolling start, so the inputs no longer fit the car's path

A normal race has no re-sync point (only the Arcade time trial restores a snapshot at each lap). So if anything in the start conditions or the simulation changes, the stored inputs drive a different car through a different race, and it drifts within seconds. "The replay shows a different race" is not something GT2's engine does on its own on this disc; it needs a changed database (a mod, another disc version) or wrong rules.

In the port: the disc's own recordings (attract demos, license demos, rally ghosts) play on classic physics, as GT2 runs them. Replays made in the port store which physics they were driven with (see battle and replays).

Limits

How long a stream lasts

Input styleBytes per tickMinutes
digital pad (disc streams)0.2 to 0.412 to 40
analog stick weave1.337.3
stick never at rest24.8

Analog steering changes the steering byte nearly every tick, so nearly every tick writes an entry. When the stream is full, recording stops silently and the race goes on; the replay ends at that point.

Other limits

  • Recording stops 5 s (300 fields) after the finish, 1 s in a license test. A cool-down lap is not replayed.
  • The last recorded tick is never played back; its input is zero.
  • One stream per player car; only player 1 and player 2 are recorded.
  • The Arcade time trial keeps only the last 4 laps, 4,096 bytes each.

Bugs found

C1 · The camera runs on car 5's clock

CONFIRMED

Every car's lag spring uses the last car's time step. Measured camera motion only fits this way. Cosmetic; the port's classic physics keeps it.

C3 · Analog fills the replay

CONFIRMED

Measured 1.33 bytes per tick with a scripted stick weave: a DualShock or neGcon race over about 5 to 7 minutes has a replay that ends early.

C4 · Reversed zoom limits

CONFIRMED

32 tracking cameras in 18 course files have their zoom limits the wrong way round, so the zoom jumps between two values instead of moving smoothly.

C5 · Dead TV cameras

CONFIRMED

6 records have a last lap before their first lap and can never be picked (Seattle, its reverse and 2P versions, the highway pair, the license course).

C6 · No camera collision

CAUSE FOUND

No camera queries the course except for visibility.

P8 · IB-4 demo off course

CONFIRMED

Not reproduced on this disc with IB-4's own rules. Under other start conditions the demo leaves the road; the test records a fail, but a demo prints no FAIL text. Fits a demo recorded under different conditions than the shipped test.
