Tune parts in transients

How suspension, springs, dampers, stabilizers, alignment, LSD and tires change turn-in, weaves, a lane change, power-on, lift-off and braking in a corner, on both of the port's physics: classic (GT2's own physics, whole-number math, 30 updates a second) and modern (the port's rewrite in real-world units, 120 steps a second). A transient is the moment a car's state is changing: turning in, changing lanes, lifting off. Each test feeds the same fixed controller inputs to both, starting from a settled car. Car codes like a2gcn are GT2's own car IDs.

The short answer

Same direction, smaller on modern. Every part that acts on both acts the same way. Modern's effects on the body are mostly a third to four fifths of classic's. Three measured causes explain nearly all of it.

1. The linear stabilizer

Modern's bar is 25-40 % of a road car's roll stiffness, GT2's 1-4 %. So a spring change moves modern's roll about half as much (Spring Rate max: -0.255 / -0.120 deg). With GT2's quadratic bar selected, modern's spring effect on roll equals classic's and the stabilizer stops acting, as in GT2.

2. The LSD lock

GT2's clutch locks only when the wheel speeds cross and lets go when the torque difference exceeds twice its torque, in whole 1/30 s ticks: at the default deceleration setting the axle cycles lock and slip on lift-off (5-26 reversals in 1.8 s). Modern's clutch takes exactly what closes the speed difference every step, so it is already locked: deceleration sensitivity max calms classic's lift-off (-0.118) and does nothing on modern (-0.001).

3. The damper scale

Modern scales GT2's damper forces by 0.6, so its body is quicker: brake-step pitch 90 % time 0.50 s against classic's 0.78 s; with the scale at 1.0, 0.74 s.

The damper settings barely act on either physics. GT2's square-root damper is fitted through its forces at 0.3 and 0.7 m/s; a setting moves both knots, so the slope at the 0.01-0.05 m/s of steering and braking changes only 10-15 % over the whole range (Mustang front bound 7.1 / 7.6 / 8.5 kN s/m at min / default / max). One row is also one axle and one direction. Their domain is curbs, bumps and landings.

The port's modern setup: load split and damper range

The results above are for modern as first built. The port's modern physics then got two changes, and this section measures them with the same fixed inputs, the same 11 cars and 5 repeats, plus six set-ups that move several damper rows at once: the anti-roll bars take part in splitting the sideways load between the wheels (GT2_MODERN_STAB_LOADS=1), and the damper settings get a wider range at low speed (GT2_MODERN_DAMPER_RANGE=wide). Both are on by default; the flags and settings guide shows how to switch them back to GT2's way. These runs still used GT2's wheel mass (see the Wheel mass page); 5.3 also shows the LSD with the real-world wheel mass.

Stock cars and every part at its defaults are unchanged. Only the damper rows differ from the earlier runs (the 8 single rows and the 6 multi-row set-ups), and the 618-car comparison of stock and fully tuned cars is identical on both physics.

5.1 The load split: already in the per-wheel suspension

On modern's per-wheel suspension the bar's push already goes into the wheel loads, and one body roll angle, with every corner's spring and bar acting on it, splits the transfer by roll stiffness. So the bars already split the load on modern, and the load-split switch changes no number on it. Measured on a skidpad (the driver holds a 50 m circle at 0.2 .. 0.7 g; the understeer angle = mean front wheel angle less L kappa at the held curvature; the gradient = its slope over 0.2-0.6 g; the Full Custom Suspension, each end's stabilizer at every step, the other end at its default; medians of 11 cars, change against the part at its defaults):

  • Direction: as real bars. A stiffer front bar takes more of the transfer at the front and adds understeer, a stiffer rear bar takes it away. With the bar out of the loads the share stays at 0.48 for every step and both ends only change the roll (and through the camber both stiffen toward oversteer): that is the "no balance" behavior with the switch off. Classic shows nothing (GT2's quadratic bar).
  • Size: small, because the tires barely care. The front share moves 0.42 .. 0.60, yet the gradient moves only +-0.02 deg/g: GT2's load curve is nearly flat over these loads (the axles' grip per newton at 0.6 g moves by under 0.1 % between front step 1 and 7: 1.5885 / 1.5885 front), so moving load between the outer and inner wheel costs the axle almost no grip. Two further measured reasons: GT2's lateral transfer at 0.6 g is 0.08-0.10 of the axle load on the Full Custom Suspension (0.14-0.17 stock; the outer minus inner over their sum), and the GT2 cars are close to neutral in the linear range (0.03-0.08 deg/g; road cars are usually 1-4 deg/g). An earlier load model gave +-13-14 % on two cars through the AI driver; here the same physics gives a similar relative size around a much smaller gradient.
  • Transients. The 100 km/h step's steady yaw gain (yaw rate per front wheel angle, median 9.55 /s at the defaults): front min / max +0.018 / -0.024, rear min / max -0.021 / +0.030 (more understeer, less yaw gain; classic 0); yaw 90 % time and overshoot within +-0.005 s / 0.01 %; power-on and lift-off path curvature within +-0.015 (the noise of the circle maneuvers). Nothing new wobbles: the weave, step and lane-change rate reversals and peaks are the same as in the first runs (the stabilizer rows match them exactly).

5.2 The damper range: what bound and rebound min / max now do

With the wide damper range, a setting's position p = (step - default) / (steps - 1) (every row: 10 steps, default 7, so p -0.667 .. +0.333) multiplies the low-speed part of GT2's curve by 2^p (each click x1.08), fading to GT2's curve above 0.1 m/s; the low-speed slope then spans about 2.4x (bound) and 2.6x (rebound) from the softest to the stiffest step, against GT2's 1.2x / 1.3x.   • The LSD's own action does not move with M3. The inside driven wheel's peak slip in the first second changes by +0.000-0.002 with every LSD at every M3 (as at the default); the LSD's lift-off effect keeps its sign and size within the circle maneuvers' noise (about +-0.02 here: the 1-way +0.08 .. +0.105, the 2-way -0.067 .. -0.099). M3 is a mass in the body's longitudinal equation; the clutch acts between the two wheels' spin steps, which M3 does not enter.
  • Indirect: through the acceleration. Less M3 mass makes the same drive force a larger acceleration (power-on longitudinal g +6.6 % at M3 0), more load moves rearward, and lift-off decelerates harder (stock lift-off tuck-in +0.217 -> +0.231 at 0.5-1 s, +0.448 -> +0.476 at 1-2 s). The power-on push of a stock car does not move past the noise (-0.198 .. -0.238). The LSDs' small power-on effect at 50 m (+0.02-0.03) is at the noise floor at every M3.
  • M1's physical form changes the lift-off more than M3 does (stock +0.217 -> +0.189: with the wheel feeling the whole tire force, lift-off engine braking slows the driven wheels less sharply), and the 2-way LSD's lift-off calming grows (-0.086 -> -0.114). The physical pair (M3 0, M1 physical) lands near the default on both.

The LSD

The tune parts test's steady skidpad saw nearly nothing from the LSD. In transients it shows on both physics, in yaw, not in wheelspin: the inside wheel's slip ratio stays under 0.1 even at full throttle in a 25 m circle and the LSD moves it by 0.001-0.004, while Initial Torque max takes about a third of the power-on push away (+0.069 / +0.064 against a stock -0.225 / -0.221), the 2-way LSD calms lift-off (-0.172 / -0.093) and the 1-way lets the car tuck in (+0.051 / +0.078). The 1-way, 1.5-way and 2-way parts are the same on power; they differ on the deceleration side, which is where the physics differ (cause 2).

The suspension kit's skidpad gap

The tune parts test measured +0.044 g on classic and +0.022 g on modern. Moving the kit's fields one group at a time: only ride height (GT2's CG height term: less load transfer, less roll) and springs (less roll) move the steady circle, on both. Modern has every one of these terms in GT2's form. Modern's smaller gain has two measured reasons: its stock car already rolls less (the linear bar), and with GT2's body forms selected modern reproduces classic's roll, grip per newton and load transfer, yet holds 1.242 g where classic holds 1.195. With the kit both reach about 1.25-1.28 g. So on classic part of the kit's gain closes a gap between the grip on offer and what the car holds on the circle; what makes classic lose the circle earlier is not traced (GT2's heading step and its 30 Hz tire update are the candidates).

What the port uses

  • Linear anti-roll bars on modern, as in real cars (GT2_MODERN_STAB_MODEL=linear; gt2 gives GT2's quadratic bar).
  • The bars take part in the load split (GT2_MODERN_STAB_LOADS=1).
  • The wide damper range, so the damper settings act on the body (GT2_MODERN_DAMPER_RANGE=wide; gt2 gives GT2's range).
  • No tuning toward classic. Classic is the ruler here, not the target: modern is not adjusted to copy classic's numbers.
